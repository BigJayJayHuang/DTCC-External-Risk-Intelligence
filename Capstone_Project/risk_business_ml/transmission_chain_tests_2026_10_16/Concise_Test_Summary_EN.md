# FICC and ECS Candidate Transmission Chain Test Summary

Execution date: October 16, 2026. Scope: the two v1 documents supplied by the team, covering 22 candidate chains.

**Results: Eight chains completed both statistical tests, and two met the hypothesis-card thresholds. The remaining chains underwent signal screening, field extraction, event checks, or case reviews, but their tests are not yet complete.** Meeting a threshold does not establish causation, validate a calibrated risk score, or demonstrate a business loss.

Following your feedback, the framework for this round is **Risk priority = External shock S × Internal vulnerability V**. News is first matched to an applicable transmission chain, and its business relevance is checked at that stage; there is no separate E multiplier. V measures buffers and weaknesses. Missing V must not be replaced with zero. This round did not produce final risk scores or retrain the existing Ridge model.

## Main Points for Revision

1. **NSCC-02A has new data support.** Cboe volume and SEC CNS outstanding FTD balances have been aligned. High-volume weeks are associated with higher outstanding fail balances, but this does not directly establish a higher fail rate or insufficient system capacity. The SEC defines the field as an outstanding balance; summing it across days does not measure new fails.
2. **NSCC-03B provisionally meets the card thresholds only.** The same-quarter correlation is ρ = 0.454, with a positive interval. Associations in changes and in the later sample are weak, and the quarterly primary tests do not pass the 5% BH threshold. Using the VM ratio suggested in the source document yields a next-quarter correlation of approximately 0.711. Because both the outcome and lag change, this remains a secondary exploration and cannot replace the v1 primary test.
3. **Retain GSD-01 and MBSD-01 as research candidates.** GSD-01 has ρ = 0.408, but its interval crosses zero. For MBSD-01, the correlation between the mortgage-spread proxy and the quarter-over-quarter change in peak VM is ρ = 0.150, below the threshold. Failure to pass must not be interpreted as evidence that no risk transmission exists.
4. **Harmonize definitions in the ECS document.** This round follows the cards' same-quarter primary specification; 01A uses prior-quarter IM as the denominator. The earlier trial's next-quarter alignment and current-quarter IM denominator are retained separately as comparisons. VIX detects 3/4 events and passes under the new R1 event set. That does not invalidate its failure under the previous event set or establish an improvement in the model.
5. **Continue using evidence cards for chains without market indicators.** Scoring rules for the clearing mandate, competition, cyber events, and GSE reform still require team design. T+1 lacks continuous monthly history, and official first-day observations suggest fail rates need not rise. A uniform quantitative score should not be forced onto these chains.

## Test Specifications and Traceability

Each primary quarterly test fixes one signal, one outcome, and same-quarter alignment. The Spearman threshold is ρ ≥ 0.3, with the lower bound of a 95% four-quarter block-bootstrap interval above zero. The repo and FTD tests use the cards' positive-coefficient HAC regression criteria. Weekly signals are compared only with thresholds based on the preceding 52 weeks; event-window boundaries do not use observations after the window. ECS R1/R2 follow their standard specification of a daily threshold based on the preceding 252 trading days. All thresholds are project research rules, not DTCC or regulatory standards.

The historical results have already been inspected, so this is an exploratory execution rather than a new blind test. Full-period results, secondary lags, and alternative signals are stored separately in the result tables; the best result found afterward does not replace the primary card. The later-period check contains only six quarters, and one additional quarter cannot establish the relationship. PQD field 4.4.3 measures stress losses exceeding initial margin, rather than gross losses or realized losses.

The CSV label **High** means only that both card thresholds were met. It is not a probability or evidence of robust causation; robustness limitations appear in the notes. This round did not assign business-scoring weights to arbitrary correlation coefficients.
